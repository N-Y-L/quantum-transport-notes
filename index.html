Quantum transport: visual notes

Neil Yuanting Li

Take a small quantum system and attach two large reservoirs. Its energy levels have become entrances and exits. Which electrons get through, which turn back, and which remain inside long enough to matter? These notes build the answer from waves, probability current, and a few small matrices.

Read the connected derivation from incoming waves to measured current, with figures available along the way. A perfectly transmitting wire still has a two-terminal resistance. A second orbital can close a conducting path. And the tiny imaginary number added to a calculation can quietly remove particles. Each is a useful puzzle.

Assumed background: basic graduate quantum mechanics and some condensed matter. No quantum field theory or Green-function transport formalism is assumed. The models are coherent, noninteracting, and small enough to inspect. Camsari, Chowdhury, and Datta give a complementary introduction from the one-electron Schrödinger equation.

For a continuous reading path, follow the connected derivation in order. It defines the response functions and contact effects used to calculate transmission and occupation. The worked explanations are visible by default; the controls let you explore the figures afterward. The annotated references point to the original literature and Datta's teaching.

Reading alongside the anyon notes

To learn what an anyon is, begin with the anyon notes. Their early construction is self-contained. When you reach their splitter calculation, two optional detours here explain how a channel carries current and how a side orbital cancels one-particle transmission. Each passage links back to the corresponding anyon calculation. These one-electron models do not derive fractional exchange statistics or two-particle coincidence probabilities.

Open, calculate, inspect

Open any experiment from the reading map above. To read offline, download the complete repository and open index.html in a browser. All controls, vector figures, and LaTeX rendering work locally without installation or internet; external sources and the companion anyon site need a connection. Each experiment has examples, a reset, and exports of its SVG figure and numerical record. All energies are in electronvolts; one spin species is counted throughout.

The README describes the models and reproducibility checks. Inspect the numerical library, independent tests, or the computed numerical checks.
