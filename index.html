Quantum transport: visual notes

Neil Yuanting Li

Take a small quantum system and attach two large reservoirs. Its energy levels have become entrances and exits. Which electrons get through, which turn back, and which remain inside long enough to matter? These notes build the answer from waves, probability current, and a few small matrices.

The approach is physical: predict an effect, adjust a parameter, then account for the result. A perfectly transmitting wire still has a two-terminal resistance. A second orbital can close a conducting path. And the tiny imaginary number added to a calculation can quietly remove particles. Each is a useful puzzle.

Assumed background: basic graduate quantum mechanics and some condensed matter. No quantum field theory or Green-function transport formalism is assumed. The models are coherent, noninteracting, and small enough to inspect. Camsari, Chowdhury, and Datta give a complementary introduction from the one-electron Schrödinger equation.

Follow the notes in order the first time. The connected derivation explains scattering, the retarded Green function, lead self-energies, the Caroli formula, and occupations. Optional derivations sit in expandable passages. The annotated references point to the original literature and Datta's teaching.
