Five small transport experiments

Make a prediction before touching a control. Each curve is calculated in your browser from the displayed model. Energies and hopping amplitudes are in eV. We count one spin species; two identical, independent spin species double current and conductance, not transmission per channel.

1. One level, two doors

A single orbital of energy \(\varepsilon_0\) is coupled to two electron reservoirs. An incoming wave can reflect or pass through the orbital. Predict: if both couplings become weak by the same factor, does the peak transmission shrink, or only become harder to hit?

In the wide-band approximation, each contact produces a constant retarded self-energy \(\Sigma_\alpha^r=-i\Gamma_\alpha/2\). A real energy shift has been absorbed into \(\varepsilon_0\). The escape widths \(\Gamma_L,\Gamma_R\) have units of energy. The retarded response and transmission are

\[ G^r(E)=\frac{1}{E-\varepsilon_0+i(\Gamma_L+\Gamma_R)/2}, \qquad T(E)=\Gamma_L\Gamma_R|G^r(E)|^2. \]

The contact widths describe escape from the orbital into extended lead states. Coherence is retained in this elastic scattering problem. At resonance, matched entry and exit widths give unit transmission; unequal widths leave a reflected wave. This is the single-level form of the scattering–Green-function relation.

For a one-dimensional incoming channel, the density of right-moving states contains \(1/v\), while the particle flux contains \(v\). Their product is \(1/h\) per energy: slower electrons are more numerous in the same energy interval. Thus \(I=(e/h)\int T(E)[f_L-f_R]\,dE\), with \(e>0\) and positive current defined for electron transfer from left to right. At zero temperature and small bias, \(G_{\rm cond}=(e^2/h)T(E_F)\). Even \(T=1\) gives a finite two-terminal resistance \(h/e^2\). Reservoir injection limits the current; this is not evidence of energy dissipation inside the elastic orbital. Datta, chapter 2; Datta and coauthors, introduction.

Recover the peak and the lifetime

At \(E=\varepsilon_0\), \(T_{\max}=4\Gamma_L\Gamma_R/(\Gamma_L+\Gamma_R)^2\leq1\). For total width \(\Gamma=\Gamma_L+\Gamma_R\), the isolated pole is \(\varepsilon_0-i\Gamma/2\). For a one-electron state initially localized on the orbital, the return amplitude is proportional to \(e^{-\Gamma t/(2\hbar)}\) and survival probability \(e^{-\Gamma t/\hbar}\). The amplitude e-folding time is \(2\hbar/\Gamma\); the probability e-folding time is \(\hbar/\Gamma\). These are exact for the ideal wide-band pole. They need not describe all decay near a lead band edge. Reservoir injection can maintain a steady occupation; this survival probability is not the occupation n under bias.

2. Let the leads continue

Replace the orbital by \(N\) sites with nearest-neighbor hopping \(-t\). Attach a semi-infinite chain to each end. If all on-site energies and bonds match, drawing a box around \(N\) sites cannot create a scatterer. Predict: will changing the box length alter the transmission?

\[ H_{nn}=\varepsilon_d,\quad H_{n,n+1}=-t,\qquad \Sigma^r(E)=\tau^2 g_s^r(E),\quad g_s^r(E)=\frac{E-i\sqrt{4t_\ell^2-E^2}}{2t_\ell^2} \quad (|E|<2t_\ell). \]

The lead hopping is \(t_\ell=1\) eV and its on-site energy is zero. The contact hopping is \(-\tau\). The surface Green function \(g_s^r\) is the amplitude response at the end of the lead. Eliminating lead amplitudes from the Schrödinger equation gives \(\Sigma^r=\tau^2g_s^r\): amplitude leaves the device, propagates in the lead, and returns. Its negative imaginary part also records the outward flux. Outside the lead band the self-energy is real and there is no propagating channel. Derivation and causal branch; Datta tutorial, contact self-energy example.

Weak contacts partly reflect the wave at both ends. Repeated round trips interfere: energies that fit the chain can transmit strongly, while energies between those resonances cannot. Moving an on-site energy changes the propagation phase and the band alignment. A discrete spectrum alone does not tell us the current; the way those states connect to the leads matters.

From a resolvent to the Caroli formula

With the leads eliminated, \(G^r=[(E+i0^+)I-H-\Sigma_L^r-\Sigma_R^r]^{-1}\), \(G^a=(G^r)^\dagger\), and \(\Gamma_\alpha=i(\Sigma_\alpha^r-\Sigma_\alpha^a)\). An injected incoherent mixture is weighted by \(\Gamma_\alpha\), propagates through \(G^r\), and is collected by the other lead. The probability sum is a trace:

\[ T_{L\leftarrow R}=\operatorname{Tr}[\Gamma_LG^r\Gamma_RG^a]. \]

The rightmost contact inside \(G^r\Gamma_RG^a\) injects from R; L collects. With two physical terminals, no absorption, and flux conservation, the total transmission is equal in the reverse direction, even without assuming time-reversal symmetry. For these real hopping Hamiltonians the reciprocity is also explicit. Contact matrices have nonzero entries only at the endpoints, so this trace reduces to \(\Gamma_L\Gamma_R|G^r_{1N}|^2\). See Caroli et al. (1971) and the full argument.

3. A detour that stops traffic

A second orbital is attached to the conducting orbital but has no lead of its own. Nothing random happens there. Predict: when its energy equals the incident energy, does this extra place to visit help transmission?

\[ H=\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon_0&-v\\-v&\varepsilon_s\end{pmatrix}, \qquad G^r_{00}= \frac{E-\varepsilon_s} {(E-\varepsilon_0+i\Gamma/2)(E-\varepsilon_s)-v^2}. \]

For \(v\ne0\), the conducting-site amplitude vanishes at \(E=\varepsilon_s\), so \(T=0\). The side-orbital equation at that energy forces \(v\psi_0=0\). Direct propagation and excursions into the side orbital cancel. This is a simple side-coupled Fano antiresonance: a coherent zero, not a short lifetime or a thermal average.

The limit is worth a second look: at any nonzero \(v\), transmission is exactly zero at \(E=\varepsilon_s\); at \(v=0\), the side orbital is disconnected and the conducting level recovers its ordinary resonance. The zero becomes arbitrarily narrow as \(v\) shrinks. Energy resolution and temperature decide whether it has an appreciable effect on a measured current.

4. Who fills the level?

A transmission curve says what an incoming electron can do. Reservoir occupations say whether an electron is supplied. Predict: at equilibrium, can the level be half full while the current vanishes? At large positive bias, which contact controls the filling?

\[ f_\alpha(E)=\frac{1}{1+e^{(E-\mu_\alpha)/(k_BT)}},\quad I=\frac{e}{h}\int T(f_L-f_R)\,dE,\quad n=\int\frac{dE}{2\pi}|G^r|^2(\Gamma_L f_L+\Gamma_R f_R). \]

Here \(\eta=0\). The partial spectral weights \(\Gamma_\alpha|G^r|^2\) count the device states supplied by each contact. At equilibrium they combine into \(A(E)f(E)\), where \(A=-2\,\mathrm{Im}\,G^r\). At sufficiently large positive bias, \(n\) approaches \(\Gamma_L/(\Gamma_L+\Gamma_R)\); rapid emptying into R prevents L from filling the orbital. This is the coherent, noninteracting steady state. Datta (2000), density matrix and current.

Finite-bias assumption: \(\mu_L=+V/2\) eV and \(\mu_R=-V/2\) eV when the numerical voltage \(V\) is in volts. The orbital energy and the wide-band self-energies are held fixed as \(V\) changes; there is no Poisson calculation, charging energy, interaction correction, or bias-induced structural change. This is an assigned voltage model, not a prediction of the internal potential of a real device. At \(\eta=0\), the currents entering from the two leads obey \(I_L+I_R=0\).

Why the missing Pauli factors are not missing

For elastic independent-electron transport, the net occupation factor is \(f_L(1-f_R)-f_R(1-f_L)=f_L-f_R\). Equal occupations cancel the two directed flows. At \(T=0\), the current is an integral over the interval between the chemical potentials; thermal broadening replaces its sharp edges by smooth weights.

5. The imaginary-number audit

The symbol \(i\eta\) can mean a retarded limiting prescription, a finite numerical smoothing, or an explicitly modeled loss. These are different claims about the same algebra. Predict: if \(\eta\) is kept finite in the device resolvent, where does the missing incident probability go?

\[ G_\eta^r=[E+i\eta-H-\Sigma_L^r(E)-\Sigma_R^r(E)]^{-1},\qquad A=G_\eta^r(\Gamma_L+\Gamma_R+2\eta I)G_\eta^a. \]

The retarded boundary condition means \(\eta\to0^+\); it selects the response after the source is applied. It does not assign a measured material lifetime. A finite positive \(\eta\), with physical lead self-energies evaluated at \(E+i0^+\), acts algebraically like an extra self-energy \(-i\eta I\), with width \(\Gamma_\eta=2\eta\). If no incoming particles are supplied with it, it is an empty absorbing reservoir. It is not a conserving model of inelastic scattering or pure dephasing. Resolvent, spectral identity, and injection derivations; Datta tutorial, scattering self-energies.

For one wide-band pole, \(e^{-\eta t/\hbar}\) is the additional amplitude damping and \(e^{-2\eta t/\hbar}\) is the additional probability damping. If this is an intentional absorber, its probability e-folding time is \(\hbar/(2\eta)\); a physical escape width \(\Gamma\) instead gives \(\hbar/\Gamma\). Here \(\hbar=0.6582119569\) eV·fs. A numerical η becomes a credible approximation only when the observable has converged as η decreases and the energy integration is also resolved.

Change η and the grid separately

The table recomputes the full spectral area \(\int A\,dE/(2\pi)\) with the selected window and alignment. The exact finite-window area is an arctangent integral. Its difference from one is a window error; the sampled area's difference from that exact value is a quadrature error. Lowering η can expose an unresolved peak rather than improve the answer.

The full spectral area is one over the infinite energy axis for this single normalized orbital. The contact part alone integrates to \(\Gamma/(\Gamma+2\eta)\), with \(\Gamma=\Gamma_L+\Gamma_R\). Algebraic current balance can hold even on a poorly resolved grid, so it does not by itself establish numerical convergence. Normalization of the full \(A\) therefore does not prove that a two-terminal current calculation is conserving. In the fixed-bias readout above, the missing current is computed from lead injection into the empty η reservoir. An isolated level (\(\Gamma=0\)) has no lead current; its η-broadened spectrum still tests the grid. Its occupation is not determined by the two physical reservoirs in the strict isolated limit.

A useful convergence order
  1. Specify the physical contact self-energies and any actual scattering model.
  2. Make η small compared with the physical widths and any feature you need to resolve.
  3. Refine the energy grid and shift its alignment; enlarge the energy window independently.
  4. Lower η again. Check observables, spectral weight, and the balance of all terminal currents.

When the spectrum contains a bound state or a narrowing antiresonance, pointwise convergence is not enough. For a discrete isolated level, the limit is a delta function: integrating it and taking η to zero are not interchangeable on a fixed grid. The exact real-axis bound pole is not an ordinary finite density value.
